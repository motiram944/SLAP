Traditional RAG has hit an architectural wall. We built SLAP to replace it.

For the past two years, building AI applications followed an identical formula:
1. Chunk your documents into text snippets.
2. Index vectors in a vector database.
3. Retrieve top-5 chunks and dump 500 to 2,000 raw text tokens directly into your prompt context.

The hidden bottleneck?
💥 GPU KV-Cache RAM explosion: On models like Llama-3-70B, boilerplate context consumes massive VRAM per request.
💥 Quadratic attention latency O(N²): Prefill compute slows down every query.
💥 Skyrocketing token bills: You pay for every single word of repetitive context.

Why are we still converting mathematical vectors BACK into paragraphs of English text just to feed them into an LLM?

Today, I’m excited to introduce SLAP (Semantic Latent-Algebraic Protocol) — an open-source alternative to traditional RAG that operates directly in continuous latent space.

Instead of stuffing raw text chunks into the prompt, SLAP:
1. Maps documents onto an orthogonal unit hypersphere S^(d-1) (d=384).
2. Quantizes residual delta vectors using INT8 (>44.6 dB SNR lossless fidelity, 3.92x storage compression).
3. Indexes vectors into high-speed Qdrant Cloud storage.
4. Injects exactly 3 continuous virtual prefix tokens directly into the LLM’s hidden space (4096-d).

The LLM attends to the exact continuous semantic representation — bypassing text prompt serialization entirely!

📊 REAL-WORLD AUDITED BENCHMARK (20-Page Technical Document):
⚡ Prompt Context: 292 tokens ➡️ 19 tokens (93.4% REDUCTION)
⚡ GPU KV-Cache RAM: 96.0 MB ➡️ 6.2 MB (15.2x LESS MEMORY)
⚡ Vector Storage: Uncompressed FP32 ➡️ INT8 Quantized (3.92x STORAGE SAVINGS)
